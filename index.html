01 — Profile

An ECE undergraduate who ships hardware, not slideware.

My work sits where embedded firmware meets real radio links. I design and deploy end-to-end hardware–software systems — from RF-based vehicle telemetry to multi-node sensor networks — on Raspberry Pi, STM32 and ESP32, using whatever wireless protocol the physics actually calls for.

Most of what I build is judged by whether it holds up in a noisy, moving, electrically hostile environment. That means I spend as much time on filtering, calibration and link reliability as on features: hard-iron compensation for magnetometers, packet-loss monitoring across MQTT nodes, corridor geometry that has to be right the first time because an ambulance is already moving.

I'm aiming at embedded system development and embedded application development — the places where firmware discipline and reliability engineering stop being separate subjects.

02 — Systems

Firmware, signal conditioning, wireless link, and the dashboard on top of it — each one taken from breadboard to something that runs unattended.

SYS/01 In development

V2V Smart Ambulance Alert System

A vehicle-to-vehicle RF alert network that broadcasts an ambulance's live position, heading and route corridor to nearby vehicles, so civilian drivers are warned before the siren is audible.

  • Architected the full system on ESP32-S3 (ESP-IDF), broadcasting live GPS position, heading and route corridor over ESP-NOW to receiver units in real time.
  • Built a three-stage magnetometer filtering pipeline — exponential low-pass, circular moving average, deadband suppression — with continuous hard-iron calibration, to hold heading stable in electrically noisy vehicle cabins.
  • Developed live Leaflet.js dashboards with WebSocket telemetry streaming, animated route tracking and corridor-membership geometry to fire accurate "Ambulance Approaching" alerts.
  • Leading migration to a production-grade Raspberry Pi 4 architecture with nRF24L01 links, designed around India's AIS-230 compliance timeline and the DoT spectrum exemption for V2X.
  • Currently advancing through the IEEE EDS PMEC Edge AI Hackathon 2026.
